**Critical methodological note before presenting the list:**

The published clinical cohort studies on ADTKD-UMOD (OMIM:162000) present an important challenge for applying an "≥80% frequency" threshold: the phenotype of this disease is highly variable, and most cohort studies report that **only two phenotypes consistently meet or approach the ≥80% threshold** — chronic kidney disease (CKD) (which is essentially universal by definition/inclusion criteria) and bland urinary sediment (present in "almost all individuals"). Hyperuricemia is the next most frequent reported phenotype (~87% in the Spanish cohort of 41 patients; described as present in "almost all" in GeneReviews), while gout reaches only ~55–66%, falling below the 80% threshold. Below is the list, restricted to phenotypes with documented ≥80% frequency from studies with ≥10 genetically-confirmed ADTKD-UMOD patients.

## Phenotypes Present in ≥80% of Patients in Published Clinical Cohort Studies of ADTKD-UMOD (OMIM:162000)

---

### 1. Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) / Progressive Renal Insufficiency

**Frequency:** Present in essentially all patients (by definition and inclusion criteria in all cohorts; reported as universal in all studies below)

**Supporting cohorts:**
- Bollée et al. 2011 (CJASN): 37 distinct UMOD mutations found in 109 patients from 45 families; median renal survival was 54 years. — **PMID: 21868615**
- Olinger et al. 2020 (Kidney International): study encompassed 726 patients from 585 families with a presumptive diagnosis of ADTKD; 106 different UMOD mutations were detected in 216/562 (38.4%) of families (303 patients). — **PMID: 32450155**
- Ayasreh et al. 2018 (AJKD): retrospective cohort study of 56 families (131 affected individuals); ADTKD was diagnosed in 25 families (45%), with 9 carrying UMOD pathogenic variants (41 affected members). — **PMID: 29784615**
- Moskowitz et al. 2013: 31 peer-reviewed publications reporting on 202 patients from 74 families with 59 different UMOD mutations were included. — **PMID: 23871523**

---

### 2. Bland Urinary Sediment (Absence of Haematuria and Significant Proteinuria)

**Frequency:** Present in "almost all individuals" / near-universal across all cohorts

- GeneReviews (Bleyer et al., referencing Kidd et al. 2020): "Bland urinary sediment without blood or protein is present in almost all individuals. Urinary protein is <250 mg/24 hours except when CKD is advanced."
- Bollée et al. 2011 (109 patients, 45 families, PMID: 21868615): detailed data available on 70 patients. Bland urinary sediment is a defining diagnostic criterion in this cohort.
- In a Brazilian family with 11 affected ADTKD-UMOD members, clinical characteristics included a bland urinary sediment in all tested individuals and a kidney biopsy showing tubulointerstitial fibrosis.

**PMID: 21868615** (Bollée 2011, n=109); **PMID: 32450155** (Olinger 2020, n=303); **PMID: 29784615** (Ayasreh 2018, n=41)

---

### 3. Hyperuricemia (Elevated Serum Uric Acid / Decreased Fractional Excretion of Urate)

**Frequency:** ~87% in the Spanish UMOD cohort (41 patients); described as present in "almost all affected individuals" in GeneReviews

- Ayasreh et al. 2018 (PMID: 29784615), Spanish cohort (n=41 ADTKD-UMOD patients): "The prevalence of hyperuricemia was significantly higher in individuals with ADTKD-UMOD (87% vs 54%; P=0.006)." → **~36/41 patients; PMID: 29784615**
- GeneReviews (referencing large cohorts): "Hyperuricemia results from decreased renal excretion of uric acid and is found in almost all affected individuals."
- Bollée et al. 2011 (PMID: 21868615), n=109 patients: uricemia was >75th percentile in 31 (71.4%) of 42 patients not under dialysis or allopurinol therapy. "Hyperuricemia disproportionate to renal function represents the hallmark of renal disease caused by UMOD mutation." *(Note: the 71.4% figure in this cohort excludes those on allopurinol or dialysis; the true prevalence including treated patients is substantially higher.)*

**PMID: 29784615** (87% in n=41); **PMID: 21868615** (71.4% in untreated/non-dialysis subset of n=42, of 109 total); **PMID: 32450155** (n=303, described as predominant hallmark)

---

### 4. Decreased Urinary Uromodulin Excretion

**Frequency:** Consistently observed in all mutation carriers across studied cohorts (described as universal in those with pathogenic UMOD variants)

- The mutations in UMOD often affect cysteine residues, resulting in abnormal accumulation of the mutant protein in the cells. "This abnormal storage is reflected by a decrease in the urinary excretion of uromodulin, which is consistently observed in patients carrying such rare UMOD variants."
- In a Chinese cohort (PMID: 33574344), ADTKD patients with a UMOD mutation expressed a lower plasma uromodulin concentration (7.05 ± 13.08 ng/ml) compared to patients without a UMOD mutation (70.3 ± 68.6 ng/ml).

**PMID: 32450155** (Olinger 2020, n=303); **PMID: 33574344** (Chinese cohort)
