## Search Results: Clinical Cohort Studies of Glucocorticoid Deficiency 2 (OMIM:607398)

**Important transparency note:** OMIM 607398 (Glucocorticoid Deficiency 2, *MRAP*) is a very rare disorder. After a thorough PubMed search, **only one published clinical cohort study with ≥10 FGD type 2 (MRAP mutation) patients** was identified:

---

### Chung TT, Chan LF, Metherell LA, Clark AJ.
**"Phenotypic characteristics of familial glucocorticoid deficiency (FGD) type 1 and 2."**
*Clin Endocrinol (Oxf).* 2010 May;72(5):589–94.
**PMID: 19558534** | PMC: PMC2855830

This study included 40 patients with missense MC2R mutations (FGD type 1) and **22 patients with MRAP mutations (FGD type 2)**. Of these, 44 had been referred for genetic screening and 18 were patients published by other authors.

The study is the **largest published cohort specifically comparing FGD type 1 and FGD type 2 phenotypes**. The paper's results section and Table 1 (in the full text) report phenotypic frequencies across both groups. The key finding for **FGD type 2 specifically** is as follows:

### Key Findings with Cited Support:

Forty patients with missense MC2R mutations and 22 patients with MRAP mutations were included in the study.

FGD type 2 presented at a much earlier median age (0.08 years; range at birth to 1.6 years) compared to FGD type 1 (P < 0.01), and patients were of normal height SDS +0.12 ± 1.35 (P < 0.001). No differences in baseline cortisol or ACTH levels were seen between FGD types 1 and 2.

Tall stature is associated with mutations in MC2R but not in MRAP. There were no other significant clinical distinctions between the two types.

The diagnosis of FGD is based on clinical findings; patients usually present with hypoglycaemia, seizure, jaundice, hyperpigmentation, failure to thrive and frequent or severe infections. The biochemical findings are a markedly elevated plasma ACTH in the presence of low cortisol but with a preserved mineralocorticoid production.
