## Holt-Oram Syndrome — Phenotypes Present in ≥80% of Patients

### Study 1: Newbury-Ecob et al. 1996 — *J Med Genet* (n = 55 cases)
**PMID: [8730285](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8730285/)**

A clinical and genetic study of 55 HOS cases was carried out in the United Kingdom. Data from both familial and isolated cases were used to define the HOS phenotype. Skeletal defects affected the upper limbs exclusively and were bilateral and asymmetrical. All affected cases showed evidence of upper limb involvement. Cardiac defects were seen in 95% of familial cases and included both atrial septal defect (ASD, 34%) and ventricular septal defect (VSD, 25%); 39% had only ECG changes.

| Phenotype | Frequency | PMID |
|---|---|---|
| Upper limb malformation (any) | 55/55 (100%) | 8730285 |
| Any cardiac involvement (structural CHD + ECG/conduction changes) | ~52/55 (95%) — familial cases | 8730285 |

Limb defects were found in all affected persons. In most cases, the thumb defects (absence in 19/44, hypoplasia in 17/44, triphalangeal thumbs in 8/44) were associated with hypoplastic thenar or limited supination of the forearm.

> **Note:** For the familial cohort of 44 cases within this study, cardiac involvement was 95% (≥80% threshold met). For the full 55-case cohort, upper limb involvement was 100%.

### Study 2: Vanlerberghe et al. 2019 — *Eur J Hum Genet* (n = 78 patients with TBX5 variants)
**PMID: [30552424](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30552424/)**

The authors retrospectively reviewed 212 patients referred for suspicion of HOS, of whom 78 had an identified TBX5 variant, representing the largest molecular series ever described. A cardiac defect was observed in 91% (71/78) of the patients. The upper limb defects were bilateral in 96% (75/78) of the patients. A left/right asymmetry was observed in 91% (31/34) of the patients when this information was available.

A CHD was present in 91% of the patients with a TBX5 variant, atrial septal defects being the most common (61.5%). The genotype–phenotype study highlights the importance of the septal characteristic of the CHD, the bilateral and asymmetric characteristics of the radial defect, and the presence of shoulder or elbow mobility defect.

| Bilateral upper limb defects | 75/78 (96%) | 30552424 |
| Left > right asymmetry of upper limb defects | 31/34 (91%) | 30552424 |
| Any congenital heart disease or cardiac involvement | 71/78 (91%) | 30552424 |

### Study 3: Basson et al. 1994 — *N Engl J Med* (n = 37 affected members across 2 families)
**PMID: [8114858](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8114858/)**

The syndrome is transmitted as an autosomal dominant trait that is highly penetrant. Upper-limb anomalies are always present. These may be unilateral or bilateral and involve structures derived from the embryonic radial ray, typically the radial, carpal, and thenar bones. Aplasia, hypoplasia, fusion, and anomalous development of these structures produce a wide spectrum of phenotypes including triphalangeal or absent thumbs, foreshortened arms, and phocomelia.

In Family A (19 affected members), all had mild-to-moderate skeletal deformities including triphalangeal thumbs and carpal-bone dysmorphism. In Family B (18 affected members), all had moderate-to-severe skeletal deformities including phocomelia; 12 had no cardiac defects and 6 had only atrial septal defects.

| Phenotype | Frequency | PMID |
|---|---|---|
| Upper limb malformation (any) | 37/37 (100%) | 8114858 |
| Carpal bone dysmorphism | 37/37 (100%) | 8114858 |
