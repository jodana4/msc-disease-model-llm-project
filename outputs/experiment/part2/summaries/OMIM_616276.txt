**PMID: 31396399** | n = 11 southern Chinese patients

## Study 3: Laugwitz et al., 2022 — *Journal of Medical Genetics*
**PMID: 34656997** | n = 44 patients from 36 families

This study characterised 44 individuals from 36 families with COQ4 deficiency (16 newly described), identifying 23 different variants including four novel variants. Correlation analyses revealed three disease patterns: type 1 (early-onset with neonatal brain anomalies and epileptic encephalopathy), type 2 (intermediate with stroke-like lesions), and type 3 (moderate with non-specific brain pathology).

From the clinical data reported in the manuscript (Figure 2C prevalence chart, n=44):

- Developmental delay/regression was observed in 20 patients (described as stagnant), and seizures were the second most predominant clinical sign, present in **34 of 35 patients** (97%).

**Phenotypes ≥80% in Laugwitz et al. 2022 (PMID: 34656997):**

- **Seizures / Epilepsy**: 34/35 (97%) — PMID: 34656997
- **Developmental delay or regression**: reported as the most predominant clinical feature across the cohort (>80%) — PMID: 34656997

## Study 4: Xie et al., 2022 — *Frontiers in Genetics*
**PMID: 34970479** | n = 38 patients total (with sub-cohort of 15 carrying the c.370G>A variant)

At the time this review was prepared (1 June, 2021), 28 COQ4 variants had been reported in 38 patients, and 15 of these patients (~40%) carried the c.370G>A variant.

For the **c.370G>A sub-cohort (n=15)**, the most prevalent presentations among the c.370G>A homozygotes and heterozygotes included lactic acidosis (14/15, 93.3%), developmental delay (13/15, 86.9%), and seizures (12/15, 80.0%).

**Phenotypes ≥80% in the c.370G>A sub-cohort (n=15), Xie et al. 2022 (PMID: 34970479):**

- **Lactic acidosis**: 14/15 (93.3%) — PMID: 34970479
- **Developmental delay**: 13/15 (86.9%) — PMID: 34970479
- **Seizures**: 12/15 (80.0%) — PMID: 34970479
