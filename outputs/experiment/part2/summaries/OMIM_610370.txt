Disease: Diarrhea 4, malabsorptive, congenital (OMIM:610370)
============================================================
RESEARCH FINDINGS:
This is an extremely rare disease. I have now gathered sufficient data from the literature. The key finding is that **only one study** — Rubio-Cabezas et al., 2014 (PMID: 25120094), published in *Diabetologia* — aggregated a cohort of **≥10 patients** (n=11: 4 new patients plus a review of 7 previously reported cases) with DIAR4 (OMIM:610370) and systematically described phenotypic features across them. All other published reports are individual case reports or small series of ≤4 patients.

Below is a detailed assessment of that study's findings, cross-referenced with the full-text data accessible via PubMed Central (PMC4181041):

---

## Important Caveat

DIAR4 (OMIM:610370) is an **extremely rare disorder**. As of the most recent systematic summary (Wejaphikul et al., 2022, PMID: 36149814), biallelic pathogenic variants in NEUROG3 cause malabsorptive diarrhea, insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus, and rarely hypogonadotropic hypogonadism — with only 17 reported cases in the world literature at that time.

The **only published study** meeting your criterion of **≥10 patients** is:

> **Rubio-Cabezas O et al.** "Neurogenin 3 is important but not essential for pancreatic islet development in humans." *Diabetologia.* 2014 Nov;57(11):2421–4. **PMID: 25120094**

This study reported 4 new patients from 3 families with DIAR4 and reviewed the 7 previously reported cases, making a total of 11 patients. Early-onset severe malabsorptive diarrhea was a common finding in all 11 patients, with enteric anendocrinosis demonstrated in the 7 patients who underwent intestinal biopsies.

---

## Phenotypes Present in ≥80% of Patients (from Rubio-Cabezas et al., 2014; n=11; PMID: 25120094)

1. **Congenital malabsorptive diarrhea (early-onset severe)**
 — Frequency: **11/11 (100%)**
 — PMID: 25120094
 "Early-onset severe malabsorptive diarrhoea was a common finding in the 11 patients."

2. **Enteric anendocrinosis (absence/paucity of intestinal enteroendocrine cells on biopsy)**
 — Frequency: **7/7 (100%) of those biopsied** (biopsy not performed in all 11)
 — PMID: 25120094
 Enteric anendocrinosis was demonstrated in all cases where this condition was investigated on intestinal biopsies; absence of enteroendocrine cells was not specifically investigated in the four patients included in the new study, but had previously been reported in patients with the same NEUROG3 mutations.

3. **Requirement for parenteral nutrition**
 — Frequency: **11/11 (100%)** among previously reviewed cases
 — PMID: 25120094
 Ten patients with homozygous NEUROG3 mutations had been reported previously, and each patient suffered from severe congenital malabsorptive diarrhea necessitating parenteral nutrition.

4. **Diabetes mellitus (insulin-dependent, variable age of onset)**
 — Frequency: **≥10/11 (~91%)** across the combined cohort (one patient had not yet developed diabetes at time of report, but later did)
 — PMID: 25120094
 No evident genotype–phenotype correlation exists between NEUROG3 mutations and pancreatic endocrine function; NEUROG3 deficiency can lead to a number of different phenotypes of diabetes, including permanent neonatal diabetes, relapsing transient neonatal diabetes, and childhood-onset permanent diabetes.

**Note on other phenotypes:** Short stature and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism were prominently described in the Rubio-Cabezas 2016 follow-up series (PMID: 27552703, n=4 patients) and in Wejaphikul et al. 2022 (PMID: 36149814, n=3 patients), but these series each included <10 patients and thus do not meet your inclusion threshold. Pancreatic exocrine insufficiency was present in only a subset of the 11-patient cohort and does not reach the 80% threshold. No other published cohort study of DIAR4 with ≥10 patients has been identified in the literature.
