### Important Caveats

- There was no description of extrarenal symptoms in some patients, and it was unclear whether they had any extrarenal symptoms or whether their extrarenal symptoms had been assessed — meaning frequency data for ocular and neuromuscular features in the Suzuki 2023 cohort (PMID 37705905) are likely underestimates due to missing data.
- In 2010, Matejas et al. showed a genotype-phenotype correlation in their systematic review of 51 previously reported cases, confirming that patients with biallelic truncating variants have severely affected phenotypes, whereas those with at least one missense variant had mild phenotypes. This means phenotype frequencies are highly dependent on mutation type, making a single aggregate frequency figure potentially misleading.
- Although patients have mutations in the same gene, the phenotype is highly variable.

In conclusion, **proteinuria/nephrotic syndrome (in any form) is the only phenotype consistently reported in 100% of patients across all qualifying cohort studies** for NPHS5 (OMIM:614199). The Mohney et al. 2011 family cohort (PMID 21236492) additionally documents 100% frequency of chronic kidney disease and bilateral chorioretinal pigmentary changes in their 11-patient family series, representing a specific missense-variant phenotypic subtype.
