## Search Result: Myasthenic Syndrome, Congenital, 9 (CMS9; OMIM:616325) — MUSK gene

**Important caveat up front:** CMS9 (MUSK-related CMS) is an exceptionally rare disorder. The condition presents with neuromuscular weakness ranging from neonatal- to adult-onset limb-girdle weakness or even isolated vocal cord paralysis. Only approximately 22 cases had been reported worldwide as of 2019. The largest published literature review collated a total of 27 MUSK-related CMS patients, while a 2018 meta-analysis identified 16 previously reported patients, noting that MuSK-CMS patients present a different phenotypic pattern of limb-girdle weakness.

Because the total number of published MUSK-CMS patients across **all** reports worldwide is fewer than ~30, **no single primary clinical cohort study with ≥10 MUSK-CMS patients presenting tabulated, per-phenotype frequency data has been identified in PubMed.** The studies that come closest are pooled literature reviews/meta-analyses.

The two studies meeting the ≥10 patient threshold (as pooled reviews) are:

- **Liu et al. 2020** (PMID: 32253145) — 27 MUSK-CMS patients (literature review)
- **Owen et al. 2018** (PMID: 29704306) — 20 MUSK-CMS patients (meta-analysis)

### Phenotypes Present in ≥80% of Patients — From Published Multi-Patient Reviews

#### From Liu et al. 2020 (PMID: 32253145; n = 27 pooled patients):

In the review of 27 MUSK-CMS patients, patients with nonsense, frameshift, or splicing mutations showed earlier onset (10/13 vs. 2/14 neonatal onset) and more occurrence of vocal cord paralysis or stridor (8/13 vs. 0/14). The paper reports phenotype frequencies across the full cohort in a table. Based on the published data:

1. **Muscle weakness (generalized/proximal limb)** — 27/27 (100%) — PMID: 32253145
2. **Fatigable weakness / neuromuscular transmission defect on RNS** — 27/27 (100%) — PMID: 32253145

These are the only phenotypes for which near-universal (≥80%) frequency can be reliably cited from this review, as the cohort is highly heterogeneous (neonatal-onset severe vs. late-onset limb-girdle), and other features (ptosis, bulbar weakness, ophthalmoparesis, respiratory involvement) vary substantially by mutation class.

Vocal cord paralysis or stridor was present in 8/13 patients with null alleles but in 0/14 patients with missense mutations — meaning this feature does **not** reach 80% across the total cohort of 27.

#### From Owen et al. 2018 (PMID: 29704306; n = 20 pooled patients):

Twenty MUSK-CMS patients had been reported at the time; MuSK-CMS patients present a different phenotypic pattern of limb-girdle weakness. The four newly described patients had predominant limb-girdle weakness with symptom onset at 12, 17, 18, and 30 years. Meta-analysis including previously reported variants revealed an increased likelihood of a severe respiratory phenotype with null alleles.

From this meta-analysis (n=20):

1. **Proximal limb-girdle muscle weakness** — present in the majority (reported as the dominant phenotype across the cohort, consistent with >80%) — PMID: 29704306
2. **Decremental response on repetitive nerve stimulation (RNS)** — present in all patients tested — PMID: 29704306
