## Phenotypic Features of MRKH Syndrome (OMIM:277000) Present in ≥80% of Patients

The following phenotypes were reported at ≥80% frequency in published clinical cohort studies with ≥10 patients. Only phenotypes for which a specific numerical frequency was reported (or definitionally universal) are included.

---

### 1. **Vaginal aplasia / complete upper vaginal atresia**
In Oppelt PG et al. (2012), complete atresia of the vagina (V5b) was found in **284/284 patients (100%)** of 284 classifiable women with MRKH syndrome.
- **PMID: 22943664**

---

### 2. **Cervical aplasia / bilateral cervical atresia**
Complete bilateral atresia of the cervix (C2b) was found in **284/284 patients (100%)** in the same cohort of 284 women.
- **PMID: 22943664**

---

### 3. **Uterine aplasia / bilateral rudimentary or aplastic uterine horns**
Bilateral rudimentary or aplastic uterine horns were found in **239/284 women (84.2%)** in Oppelt PG et al. (2012).
- **PMID: 22943664**

---

### 4. **Normal adnexa (ovaries and tubes)**
Normal adnexa were found in **248/284 women (87.3%)** in the same cohort of 284 women with MRKH syndrome.
- **PMID: 22943664**

---

### 5. **Primary amenorrhea**
In a cohort study of MRKH patients, the most common presenting symptom was primary amenorrhea, reported in **88.5%** of patients. This phenotype is also universally reported as the presenting feature across all major cohort studies.
- Supporting large cohort: **PMID: 27349924** (Pan & Luo 2016, n=594); **PMID: 33745726** (Chen et al. 2021, n=1,055)

---

### 6. **Normal female karyotype (46,XX)**
In the Kapczuk et al. 2016 cohort of 125 women, the vast majority (**96%**) of patients with MRKH syndrome had a normal female karyotype 46,XX.
In the Pan & Luo 2016 cohort of 594 patients, the major karyotype of MRKH patients was 46,XX, with abnormal karyotypes found in only two cases.
- **PMID: 27825026** (Kapczuk 2016, 120/125 = 96%); **PMID: 27349924** (Pan & Luo 2016, ~592/594 = 99.7%)

---

### 7. **Normal secondary sexual characteristics (normal breast and pubic hair development)**
In the Pietzsch et al. 2024 cohort of 469 patients, as the ovaries remain hormonally functional, patients with MRKH syndrome exhibit **normal secondary sexual characteristics** with regular pubarche and thelarche during puberty. This is reported as a universal finding across all published cohorts used as part of the diagnostic definition.
- **PMC10816094** / PMID referenced in **J Clin Med 2024;13(2):607**; also universally confirmed in **PMID: 22943664**, **PMID: 27349924**, **PMID: 33745726**, **PMID: 27825026**

---

### Important caveats and notes:

> **Note on phenotype frequencies:** MRKH syndrome is *defined* by uterovaginal aplasia in a 46,XX female with normal secondary sexual characteristics and primary amenorrhea — meaning these features are present by diagnostic definition in virtually 100% of cases across all cohorts. However, for strict numerical documentation, the most rigorously quantified cohort is **Oppelt PG et al. 2012 (PMID 22943664, n=284)**, which provides the clearest per-feature percentages.
>
> Features such as normal adnexa (87.3%) and bilateral rudimentary/aplastic uterine horns (84.2%) just meet the ≥80% threshold. All other cardinal features (vaginal atresia, cervical atresia, primary amenorrhea, normal karyotype, normal secondary sexual development) are reported at or near 100% by definition in all large cohorts.
>
> Extragenital malformations (renal, skeletal, cardiac) — which are the hallmark of **Type II MRKH** — are present in only **7–55% of patients** depending on the cohort and are therefore **excluded** as they do not meet the ≥80% threshold.
