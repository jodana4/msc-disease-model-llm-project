## Search Result: No Qualifying Published Clinical Cohort Study Exists for ADTKD5 (OMIM:617056)

After a systematic search of PubMed and the published literature, **no clinical cohort study with ≥10 patients specifically describing the phenotypic features of ADTKD5 (OMIM:617056)** has been identified. Here is why, supported by the evidence found:

### Why No Qualifying Study Exists

ADTKD-SEC61A1 is a very rare disease, having been reported in only 2 families in the original publication. ADTKD–SEC61A1 is extremely rare, with only five families reported in total across the entire literature.

The disease was first defined by: Bolar et al. (2016, PMID: 27392076), who reported on two families with ADTKD and congenital anemia accompanied by either intrauterine growth retardation or neutropenia. Ultrasound and kidney biopsy revealed small dysplastic kidneys with cysts and tubular atrophy with secondary glomerular sclerosis.

The largest multi-gene ADTKD study to include SEC61A1 patients — the 2025 Japanese cohort (PMID: 39976632) — identified 52 patients from 40 families with ADTKD in total; ADTKD–UMOD was found in 24 patients (18 families), ADTKD–MUC1 in 20 patients (16 families), ADTKD–REN in 7 patients (5 families), and only 1 family with SEC61A1 — far too few to meet the ≥10-patient threshold for ADTKD5 specifically.

The only subsequent SEC61A1-specific clinical report was a **single case**: a 4-year-old girl with ADTKD5, in whom Espino-Hernandez et al. (2021, PMID: 33185949) identified a de novo heterozygous T185A mutation in the SEC61A1 gene.
